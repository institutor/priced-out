# Run summary 2026-11-22T09-26-19

**This was a practice run. An AI stand-in played Flashpoint and invented every answer. No survey was sent and no real person answered. Nothing below is a finding.**

**The objective:** Figure out the best event to put on for our club next spring.

**Given to the agent:** 8 people to ask, a $9.00 budget cap, and 2 standing orders. Nobody could be emailed more than 2 times, reminders included. Model: claude-sonnet-5-5.

**What it remembered from earlier runs:**

- Rank questions gave a clearer signal than single choice, which split on 8 people.
- Offering cost-sharing in the options changed the preference order, so test price framing explicitly.
- My guesses and the commander's both missed; ask about barriers openly instead of assuming them.
- Open-ended reasons from a small group scatter. Ranking plus 'what would bring you back' gave the usable signal.
- Two top options tied again when re-ranked. A different design, such as a forced trade-off, may be needed to split them.
- Ask openly about uncomfortable incidents. One surprise (pressure to buy) only came up because of the open question.

## What the agent decided

**Its plan, before it spent anything.**

- It needed to find out: Which event type members most prefer (ranked); What would bring them to attend / barriers (timing, cost); Any uncomfortable past incidents or dislikes
- It already suspected: A social/outdoor event ranks top; cost and timing are barriers; Acting on notes: use ranking, price framing, open barriers question
- What would change its answer: If a different option ranks top, or cost framing flips the order, or barriers make the top option unattendable.

**Round 1.**

- It sent Flashpoint 5 messages, 1 of them to write or change the survey.
- Before sending, it sealed guesses on: "Which event ranks most wanted?"; "If top event cost money, what would you do?".

- Still unknown: Workshop vs speaker tie-break; whether a hybrid (professional-led workshop) satisfies both; price tolerance for it.
- Its decision: **run another round**. Use the second email for a 3-question follow-up forcing a trade-off between workshop, speaker and a hybrid, plus price.

## The answer

**Recommendation.** Put on a practical, career/money-skills evening with a professional guest: a short talk followed by a hands-on part. Hold it on a weekday evening and keep it free or under about $10–25. Skip a social dinner (ranked last). Topics were scattered, so pick job-search/freelance/money skills, which came up most.

**Evidence.** Round 1 (8 of 8): workshop and speaker tied on average rank (2.38 each); social dinner was last (5.75, 0 firsts). Weekday evening was best for 4, Saturday daytime for 3. Cost: free-only 3, under $25 3, any cost 2, under $10 0. Several open answers cited shifts and Saturday work, cliquey socials, and wanting work-relevant sessions. Round 2 (8 of 8): guest speaker 3, combined 2, workshop 1, none 2. Max price: free 3, $10 1, $25 3, $40 1. Topics: job/freelance/hiring (3 of 8), budgeting, cars, outdoor skills, a competition night, small-group mixing.

**Limits.** Only 8 people; direction, not measurement. The tie was not cleanly broken, 2 of 8 chose none, and topics scattered. Flashpoint replies were labelled as practice stand-ins. No personas were run. Commander gave no guesses.

**What it wrote down for its next run.**

- My guess of a social event on top was wrong; don't assume social events are favoured, and include practical/career options.
- Adding a hybrid option after a tie did not decide it; with 8 people, use a forced trade-off or ranking in the first round.
- Open answers revealed barriers (shift work, cliquey socials) that closed questions missed; keep an open barrier question.
