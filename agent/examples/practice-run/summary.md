**This was a practice run. An AI stand-in played Flashpoint and invented every answer. No survey was sent and no real person answered. Nothing below is a finding.**

**The mission:** We want more members at our weekly meetings. What is the one change that would bring the most people back?

**Given to the agent:** 3 people to ask, a $8.00 budget cap, and 4 standing orders. Model: claude-sonnet-5-5.

## What the agent decided

**Round 1.**

- It sent Flashpoint 5 messages, 1 of them to write or change the survey.
- It rehearsed the survey on AI personas first. Their answers are synthetic.
- Before sending, it sealed guesses on: "Which ONE change would make you most likely to come back more often?".
- The commander approved, and the survey was emailed.
- Who was wrong: Me: I guessed day/time would win; it got 1 of 3 and tied with two others. Personas also guessed a spread but included options (topics, communication) no real person chose. Commander gave no guess.
- Biggest surprise: A travel-cost bursary: nobody, including me and the personas, had cost of getting there as an option.
- Still unknown: Whether the three barriers (time, length/frequency, cost) can be ranked, and whether any single change would help more than one person.
- Its decision: **run another round**. Round 2 asks only: rank the three surfaced changes and say if each would get them to come, to break the tie.

**Round 2.**

- It sent Flashpoint 4 messages, 1 of them to write or change the survey.
- Before sending, it sealed guesses on: "Which change would you rank first: different day/time, fewer/shorter meetings, or travel bursary?".
- The commander approved, and the survey was emailed.
- Who was wrong: Nobody badly: my guess of a split with day/time slightly ahead matched. The ranking did give day/time the best overall position.
- Biggest surprise: Everyone still ranked their own earlier pick first, and nobody said No. Each change would help someone.
- Still unknown: Whether the pattern holds beyond 3 people, and how many members are lost to each barrier.
- Its decision: **finish**. The panel is only 3 people. Another round can't give a firmer answer, and the remaining question needs a wider audience than this panel.

## The answer

**Recommendation.** Try a different meeting day or time first. It was the best-placed change overall (ranked 1st, 2nd and 2nd), although only 1 of 3 people ranked it first. The result is close to a three-way tie. Shorter, tighter meetings and a travel bursary each had one first-place vote, and no one said their top change wouldn't help. If you can do a second change cheaply, look at cost of travel, which none of our options covered.

**Evidence.** Round 1 (3 of 3 answered): different day/time 1, shorter meetings 1, Other (travel bursary for cost) 1, and 0 for every other option. Round 2 (3 of 3 answered), ranking: day/time was 1st once and 2nd twice, bursary was 1st, 2nd and 3rd, and shorter/focused was 1st once and 3rd twice. Asked whether their top change would make them come more often: 1 said yes definitely, 2 said probably, 0 said no.
