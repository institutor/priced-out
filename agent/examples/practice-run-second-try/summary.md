# Run summary 2026-11-22T09-39-55

**This was a practice run. An AI stand-in played Flashpoint and invented every answer. No survey was sent and no real person answered. Nothing below is a finding.**

**The objective:** Figure out the best event to put on for our club next spring.

**Given to the agent:** 8 people to ask, a $9.00 budget cap, and 2 standing orders. Nobody could be emailed more than 2 times, reminders included. Model: claude-sonnet-5-5.

**What it remembered from earlier runs:**

- Offering cost-sharing in the options changed the preference order, so test price framing explicitly.
- My guesses and the commander's both missed; ask about barriers openly instead of assuming them.
- Open-ended reasons from a small group scatter. Ranking plus 'what would bring you back' gave the usable signal.
- Two top options tied again when re-ranked. A different design, such as a forced trade-off, may be needed to split them.
- Ask openly about uncomfortable incidents. One surprise (pressure to buy) only came up because of the open question.
- My guess of a social event on top was wrong; don't assume social events are favoured, and include practical/career options.
- Adding a hybrid option after a tie did not decide it; with 8 people, use a forced trade-off or ranking in the first round.
- Open answers revealed barriers (shift work, cliquey socials) that closed questions missed; keep an open barrier question.

## How much it did alone

The agent took **20 steps on its own**. It did not stop for a person once. The code asked it once to think again before it stopped.

## What the agent decided

**Its plan, before it spent anything.**

- It needed to find out: Which event type members prefer (ranked), including practical/career options; What barriers (time, cost, cliques) keep them from attending; What would bring them to an event; price framing
- It already suspected: Social event is not necessarily top; practical/skill workshop may win; Cost and scheduling are barriers
- What would change its answer: A clear ranking favouring a different type, or a barrier like shift work that makes timing the real decider.

**Round 1.**

- It sent Flashpoint 5 messages, 1 of them to write or change the survey.
- Before sending, it sealed guesses on: "Which spring event type is ranked top?"; "Cost preference?".
- The code checked the survey in the commander's place, and it was emailed.
- Who was wrong: I was right on workshop leading, wrong on cost: small fee did not win; 3 want free only and money is tight for several.
- Biggest surprise: Open answers: members feel decisions are made before they're asked, and cost, shifts and not knowing anyone are barriers.
- Still unknown: Which skill topic, and which format details (free, daytime, small groups) they'd accept.
- Its answer at this point: A free or cheap weekend/daytime small-group skills workshop.
- It first chose to stop here. The loop asked it once to think again, because a question was still open and there was budget left.
- Its decision: **run another round**. Short follow-up asking members to shape the workshop (topic, format), which also answers the feeling of not being consulted.

**Round 2.**

- It sent Flashpoint 5 messages, 1 of them to write or change the survey.
- Before sending, it sealed guesses on: "Which workshop format would you most likely attend?".
- The code checked the survey in the commander's place, and it was emailed.
- Who was wrong: I partly missed: weekend free tied with a recorded/hybrid option at 3 each; I did not expect the hybrid to rival it.
- Biggest surprise: Recording demand (several raised it unprompted) and that a career-flavoured, practical topic (CV/interview) recurs.
- Still unknown: Exact topic; no emails left to ask.
- Its answer at this point: Free, small-group practical skills workshop (CV/interview/career) on a weekend daytime, recorded for others, accessible without a car.
- Its decision: **finish**. Everyone has used both emails, so no further survey can be sent.

## The answer

**Recommendation.** Put on a free, practical skills workshop next spring, in small groups on a weekend daytime. Make it career-flavoured (CV and interview technique, ideally led by people who have hired from the club), and record it for people who can't attend. Keep it open to all levels, reachable without a car, and tell members how their input shaped it.

**Evidence.** Round 1 (8 of 8 answered): skills workshop had the best average rank (2.13) and 3 first places. The other five options got 1 first place each. Cost: 3 free only, 3 fee fine if worth it, 2 small fee, 0 subsidy. Times: weekend daytime 5, weekday daytime 4, weekday evening 3, weekend evening 2. Open answers named barriers: shifts, money, not knowing anyone, transport, and feeling the committee decides before asking. Round 2 (8 of 8): free weekend small groups 3, online/recorded plus short in-person 3, free weekday daytime 1, weekday evening small fee 1. Topics were mostly practical or career skills (CV/interview came up several times).

The agent marked these itself, its own guesses included. With 8 people asked, the scoreboard shows how the method works. It does not show who is the better guesser.

**What changed between rounds.** Round 1 showed the workshop in front but also barriers (cost, shifts, feeling unconsulted, transport). So round 2 asked about workshop format and topic. It found a tie between weekend in-person and recorded/hybrid, plus a lot of interest in career skills.

**Limits.** Only 8 people, so this shows a direction and not a measurement. The workshop's lead is modest: 3 first places. The round 2 format question tied. Topic is not settled, and no emails are left to settle it. The Flashpoint replies were labelled as practice, so the results should be treated with that caveat.

**What it wrote down for its next run.**

- Ranking worked better than single choice for the main question, but single-choice format questions tied again with 8 people; use ranking everywhere.
- My cost guess was wrong; people on tight budgets want free options, so test price explicitly.
- Open barrier questions again brought up things closed questions missed (feeling unconsulted, transport, recordings).
