# Run summary 2026-11-22T09-41-40

**This was a practice run. An AI stand-in played Flashpoint and invented every answer. No survey was sent and no real person answered. Nothing below is a finding.**

**The objective:** Our club's newsletter gets opened by almost nobody. Find out what members would actually read, and how often.

**Given to the agent:** 8 people to ask, a $9.00 budget cap, and 2 standing orders. Nobody could be emailed more than 2 times, reminders included. Model: claude-sonnet-5-5.

**What it remembered from earlier runs:**

- Two top options tied again when re-ranked. A different design, such as a forced trade-off, may be needed to split them.
- Ask openly about uncomfortable incidents. One surprise (pressure to buy) only came up because of the open question.
- My guess of a social event on top was wrong; don't assume social events are favoured, and include practical/career options.
- Adding a hybrid option after a tie did not decide it; with 8 people, use a forced trade-off or ranking in the first round.
- Open answers revealed barriers (shift work, cliquey socials) that closed questions missed; keep an open barrier question.
- Ranking worked better than single choice for the main question, but single-choice format questions tied again with 8 people; use ranking everywhere.
- My cost guess was wrong; people on tight budgets want free options, so test price explicitly.
- Open barrier questions again brought up things closed questions missed (feeling unconsulted, transport, recordings).

## What the agent decided

**Its plan, before it spent anything.**

- It needed to find out: What content members would actually read (ranked); Preferred frequency; Barriers: why they don't open now (channel, subject, length, timing)
- It already suspected: Practical items (dates, schedule changes) beat stories/social; Monthly or less is preferred; weekly is too much; Barriers may be inbox overload or irrelevance; open question needed
- What would change its answer: Members prefer a different channel (chat/text) over email, or want social content over practical.

**Round 1.**

- It sent Flashpoint 5 messages, 1 of them to write or change the survey.
- Before sending, it sealed guesses on: "Which content type is ranked top?"; "Preferred frequency?".
- The code checked the survey in the commander's place, and it was emailed.
- Who was wrong: Me: I expected event dates to top; decisions/club news won (3 of 5 first places). Frequency guess was roughly right but the three non-weekly options are tied again.
- Biggest surprise: Open answers are about trust and transparency (closed committee decisions, unlabelled paid offers, same five faces), and one member wants a web page rather than a newsletter.
- Still unknown: Whether a forced trade-off settles frequency, and whether a short dates+decisions digest beats a full newsletter; 3 members silent.
- Its answer at this point: Send a short monthly (or when-needed) email centred on club decisions with reasoning plus key dates, with sponsored offers labelled; drop weekly and photo-heavy content.
- Its decision: **run another round**. Use the second email as a 3-question follow-up forcing a trade-off on frequency and format, to split the tie.

**Round 2.**

- It sent Flashpoint 5 messages, 1 of them to write or change the survey.
- Before sending, it sealed guesses on: "If only ONE, which would you read?"; "Top trust builder?".

## The answer

**Recommendation.** Replace the current newsletter with a short monthly email led by club decisions and the reasoning behind them, plus key dates. Add a plain list of volunteering and career opportunities, and label sponsored offers as paid placements. Cut back member stories and photos, and don't send weekly. Send schedule changes separately on the same day.

**Evidence.** Round 1 (5 of 8 answered): club news and decisions averaged rank 2.0 with 3 first places, event dates 2.0, opportunities 3.0, and member stories came last at 5.4. Frequency was close: monthly 1.8, only when important 2.0, fortnightly 2.2, weekly 4.0. Email was the top channel (1.2, 4 first places). Round 2 (8 of 8 answered): a monthly decisions-and-dates email got 4 votes, an email only when something important happens got 2, fortnightly got 1, and a web page got 1. Trust ranking: decisions with reasoning 1.63, labelled offers 2.13, published votes and dissent 2.63, wider range of members 3.63. Open answers: committee decisions are made behind closed doors, offers may be paid placements, the same few members feature in stories, and one member wanted print.

**What changed between rounds.** Round 1 showed frequency was close to a tie and that the open answers were about trust and transparency, not format. Round 2 therefore used a forced single choice on format and a ranking of trust items, which split the tie in favour of monthly.

**Limits.** There are only 8 people, and 5 answered round 1. This is a direction, not a measurement. Monthly won with 4 of 8 votes. The round 2 trust ranking is close between the top items. I did not test print or same-day alerts, and no emails were left for further questions. The Flashpoint replies were labelled as an AI stand-in practice system, so the results should be checked against real responses.

**What it wrote down for its next run.**

- Open questions surfaced the real driver (trust and transparency) that my closed options missed; keep one open barrier question in every first round.
- I guessed practical content (dates) would win, but decisions and news won. Don't assume practical content beats governance content.
- A forced single choice with a few distinct formats split a frequency tie in round 2. Use that design instead of re-ranking.
