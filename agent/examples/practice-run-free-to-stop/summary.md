# Run summary 2026-11-22T10-07-43

**This was a practice run. An AI stand-in played Flashpoint and invented every answer. No survey was sent and no real person answered. Nothing below is a finding.**

**The objective:** Figure out the best event to put on for our club next spring.

**Given to the agent:** 8 people to ask, a $12.00 budget cap, and 2 standing orders. Nobody could be emailed more than 3 times, reminders included. Model: claude-sonnet-5-5.

**What it remembered from earlier runs:**

- Adding a hybrid option after a tie did not decide it; with 8 people, use a forced trade-off or ranking in the first round.
- Open answers revealed barriers (shift work, cliquey socials) that closed questions missed; keep an open barrier question.
- Ranking worked better than single choice for the main question, but single-choice format questions tied again with 8 people; use ranking everywhere.
- My cost guess was wrong; people on tight budgets want free options, so test price explicitly.
- Open barrier questions again brought up things closed questions missed (feeling unconsulted, transport, recordings).
- Open questions surfaced the real driver (trust and transparency) that my closed options missed; keep one open barrier question in every first round.
- I guessed practical content (dates) would win, but decisions and news won. Don't assume practical content beats governance content.
- A forced single choice with a few distinct formats split a frequency tie in round 2. Use that design instead of re-ranking.

## What the agent decided

**Its plan, before it spent anything.**

- It needed to find out: Which event format members would most want next spring (ranked); Barriers to attending (time, cost, transport, cliques); Price tolerance / whether free is needed
- It already suspected: A casual social or outing wins, but governance/decision-type content may surprise me; Cost matters; free options preferred
- What would change its answer: If ranking favors a non-social format like a workshop or members' meeting, or barriers like shift work rule out weekday events.

## The answer

**Recommendation.** Put on a weeknight, small-group skills workshop next spring, with a career-practical theme (data/spreadsheets, job applications, negotiation, presentations). Charge a refundable $5 deposit, which covers both tight budgets and commitment. Recruit the three members who want to plan from the start to choose the topic and date, and keep it beginner-friendly.

**Evidence.** Round 1 (8 of 8 answered): skills workshop had average rank 1.63 and 4 first places; picnic 3.13, game day 3.13, outing 3.50, members' meeting 3.63. Price ceiling: up to $10 for 5, free only for 2, up to $25 for 1. Timing: weekday evening 3, Saturday daytime 3. Open answers raised cost, work schedules, newcomers feeling shut out, and wanting a skill for a résumé. Round 2 (8 of 8): fee preference was refundable $5 deposit 3, free 2, flat $5 2, flat $10 1. Involvement: plan from the start 3, vote 2, just attend 2, organize on the day 1. Skills wanted were scattered: data analysis x2, job applications, negotiation, presentations and grants, bike repair, game strategy, running meetings.
